### HERO

# AI can reason about almost anything. Someone still has to decide what it's reasoning about.

That decision — what counts as an object, what counts as state, which relationships matter, where one system ends and the next begins — happens *before* the model does anything. Get it wrong, and no amount of intelligence saves you.

Primitive is an applied research and engineering lab. We build AI from that decision outward — then benchmark it against the boring, cheaper way of doing things to find out whether any of it was worth it.

### THE PROBLEM

## The model was never the hard part.

Models are already extraordinary. The hard part is everything around them —

what counts as state,
what depends on what,
what to remember and what to drop,
what changes when the context does,
where uncertainty is allowed to live,
and how you'd even know if it worked.

Get that wrong and a better model just makes a broken system faster.

The breakthrough usually isn't a bigger model. It's a better map.

---

### OUR POSITION

## We think information comes first.

Not files or databases — something more primitive. **Difference.** One state that could have been another.

Enough differences interacting make a pattern. Some patterns persist. Some get stable enough that the rest of the system starts treating them as a thing — and a new thing exists. Then it happens again.

That's the whole engine. State, difference, relationship, recurrence, emergence — and around again.

> **The primitive may not be the thing. It may be the change.**

---

### THE DISTINCTION

## That's a belief. Not a result.

Primitive takes an information-first view of systems. We suspect that state, difference, relationship and change describe many systems better than starting from isolated objects does.

That position has ancestors — information theory, cybernetics, complexity science, physics, philosophy. The interpretation is ours. We have not demonstrated the informational foundations of reality, and we're not going to pretend otherwise.

What we can do is ask a smaller, more useful question: *if we build computational representations from these ideas, do they actually help?*

That one's testable. So we test it.

> **We reserve the right to speculate wildly. We do not reserve the right to call speculation evidence.**

---

### METHOD

## Contract. Trace. Expand.

The practical method underneath most of the work.

**01 — Contract.** Reduce the problem until the structure that matters becomes visible. What's actually happening? What's signal, what's noise? Where are we drawing the boundary — and which assumptions got smuggled in just because they're familiar?

**02 — Trace.** Map the system as it is: objects, states, relationships, constraints, information flows, decisions, dependencies, feedback, uncertainty, failure modes. Not to make a beautiful diagram — to know what has to stay true when the diagram becomes software.

**03 — Expand.** Build outward from the smallest representation that still preserves what matters. Add models, agents, memory, tools, interfaces — only when they earn their place.

Complexity is allowed. Unnecessary complexity isn't.

### RECURSIVE FIELD THEORY

## The framework underneath it all.

Primitive is developing **Recursive Field Theory** — RFT. It started as an irritation: systems that had nothing in common kept producing the same representational problem.

A conveyor network. A shortest-path problem. Human behaviour. Memory. Neural networks. Markets. Agent systems.

Different domains, different vocabulary — and underneath every one of them: states, possible relationships, actual interactions, changing context, recurring patterns, and larger structures emerging from those patterns.

RFT asks whether that recurrence can be formalised into something computationally useful. Its hardest question is also its simplest:

> **When does a pattern become a thing? And once it does — what can that new thing do?**

# Research

We develop and test ideas about representation, reasoning, relationships, information and emergence. Some begin as philosophy. Some become formal models. A smaller number become software. A smaller number again survive benchmarking.

This page exists so those stages never get confused with one another. Ideas should be ambitious. Evidence should be conservative.

### THE STATUS SYSTEM

Every research artifact on this site carries an epistemic status. It's not decoration — it's the contract.

**BELIEF** — A philosophical or foundational position. It may motivate research. It is not presented as demonstrated.
**OPEN QUESTION** — We don't currently know. It's recorded because the answer could change the framework.
**HYPOTHESIS** — Specific enough that an argument or experiment could support or contradict it.
**FORMALISED** — Precise enough — mathematically, computationally, procedurally — to inspect and attack. Formalisation is not correctness.
**EXPERIMENTAL** — Currently being tested. Treat results as provisional.
**REPLICATED** — Survived predefined repeat testing under stated conditions. Not universal validity.
**INDEPENDENTLY REPLICATED** — Reproduced by someone who doesn't work here. The standard we want more of.
**FALSIFIED** — The prediction didn't survive. It stays in the archive.
**SUPERSEDED** — Replaced by a newer formulation. The old one stays reachable.

Primitive works across four connected layers.

**01 — Philosophical.** What do we assume exists? What is state? What is difference? What draws a boundary? What is an observer? At what point does a pattern deserve to be treated as an object?

## 3 · RECURSIVE FIELD THEORY

### HERO

# Recursive Field Theory
## A developing framework for representing state, relationship, context, transformation and emergence.

`Status: FORMALISING / EXPERIMENTAL`

RFT is the central theoretical programme at Primitive. It is unfinished, and that's intentional. This page describes the current formulation rather than pretending the formulation has stopped moving.

---

### THE SIMPLE VERSION

A thing has a state. That state shapes some of the interactions available to it. It exists among other things. Interactions happen. Those interactions change what states the system can occupy. Patterns recur. Some recurring patterns persist. Persistent patterns become meaningful units at a higher level of description — and those new units get their own states and relationships.

The process is recursive. That's the whole idea, and everything else is an attempt to make it precise.

```
state
   ↓
possible relations
   ↓
interaction
   ↓
changed state
   ↓
recurrence
   ↓
persistent pattern
   ↓
emergent object
   ↓
new state space
```

---

### WHERE THE OBJECT STOPS BEING ENOUGH

Most useful models start by defining objects and then connecting them. For a huge range of problems, that works beautifully.

RFT is interested in the cases where it doesn't — where the relationships themselves change what the objects effectively are. Where context changes meaning. Where the observer changes the useful boundary. Where different scales expose different entities. Where history changes the current interpretation. Where an emergent pattern should itself become something you can address directly.

The claim is not that graphs, probabilities, embeddings or state machines are wrong. They obviously aren't. The question is whether some systems need a representational layer that can hold several of those perspectives at once.

---

### THE REPRESENTATIONAL CORE

The current formulation keeps apart five quantities that are usually collapsed together.

**q — semantic state.** What state is being represented — the orientation or categorical state of the entity within the current frame.

**o — context.** Where, when, and from what frame is it being observed? Spatial, temporal, observer-relative, scale-relative, boundary-relative. The same apparent object can occupy different meaningful states under different contextual coordinates.

**r — relationship.** How can states influence one another? Not a label attached after the objects exist — in some systems, a constraint on what states, transformations and future relationships are even possible.

**p — path / confidence weight.** How strongly does current evidence support a particular path or transition? Probability is not the same thing as semantic state, and we don't let them blur.

**u — uncertainty.** What's unresolved? Kept separate because low confidence, high ambiguity and missing information are genuinely different problems.

---

### THE SEMANTIC CUBE

One experimental RFT representation uses three ternary axes. Each takes −1, 0, or +1.

`3³ = 27 semantic coordinates.`

The cube isn't meant to represent every property of reality. It's a compact coordinate system — a fixed scaffold across which richer contextual detail is organised. Which is exactly why the axes have to be explicit, and why context, probability, uncertainty and relationship each stay in their own lane. That separation is the point, not a detail.

---

### ZERO IS NOT NECESSARILY NOTHING

In most systems, zero means absence, neutrality, null. RFT asks whether the centre can instead hold a branching or unresolved state — *if, else, or, while, neither, both-possible, not-yet-determined.*

The vocabulary doesn't matter. What matters is that indeterminacy itself can have structure.

> The interesting part of zero is everything that could still happen.

---

### WHEN DOES A PATTERN BECOME A THING?

Possibly the most important question in RFT.

Take a collection of components. Individually they have properties. Together they interact. Their interactions produce recurring organisation. Eventually another part of the system starts responding to that organisation *as a unit*. At that point, treating it as a list of lower-level parts becomes less useful than treating it as a new object.

A company. A traffic jam. A memory. A market. A storm. A behavioural pattern. A software service. An organism.

None of these are independent of their parts. But none of them are usefully described by enumerating those parts forever.

RFT tries to formalise **promotion** — the move from recurring relational pattern to higher-order object — and, where needed, **demotion** — the reversible decomposition back into the relations it emerged from.

---

### WHAT RFT IS NOT

It is not a proven physical theory. It is not a replacement for probability. It is not an argument that graphs are obsolete. It is not a claim that all complex systems are the same one. It is not evidence for a theory of consciousness. It is not an excuse to rename established mathematics. And it is not finished.

RFT is an attempt to build a coherent representational framework around a recurring problem. Its value will come down to whether it earns its keep against the alternatives. That burden is ours.

## 4 · EVIDENCE

### HERO

# Evidence
## A theory should be allowed to lose.

A successful demonstration is not evidence for a general claim. Wherever it's practical, we put new methods against simpler, established alternatives under controlled conditions — not because the baseline is exciting, but because beating a bad baseline proves almost nothing.

---

### THE DEFAULT QUESTION

Every time we add complexity, one question: **did it earn its place?**

A new representation can be elegant. A new agent can be impressive. A multi-model architecture can produce a gorgeous diagram. None of it matters if a smaller system gets the same result faster, cheaper and more reliably.

---

### BENCHMARK PRINCIPLES

**Freeze the question.** Define what success means before looking at the result.
**Keep the baseline boring.** Prefer the simplest credible established method.
**Hold variables still.** Same underlying model, same evidence, same evaluation conditions where possible.
**Count the cost.** Tokens, compute, latency, human intervention, infrastructure, complexity.
**Record failure.** Average accuracy isn't enough — a useful system also has to fail in ways you can understand.
**Publish the receipts.** Where licensing, privacy and commercial constraints allow: outputs, prompts, configs, data, code, procedure, analysis.

# Told You So™
## A small archive of potentially embarrassing confidence.

Predictions are cheap. Human memory is cheaper. So this page removes memory from the process.

### HOW IT WORKS

**01 — Make the claim.** Written before the result is known: the claim, the assumptions, the confidence, the evaluation criteria, the reveal condition, and where possible an expiry date.

**02 — Freeze it.** Packaged into a canonical file. From here, *that file* is the prediction — not our later recollection of it.

**03 — Seal it.** The original is encrypted so it stays private until the reveal.

**04 — Publish the commitment.** A cryptographic hash of the original goes public. It's a fingerprint. Change one character of the original and the fingerprint changes.

**05 — Let reality do whatever it wants.** Generally the inconvenient part.

**06 — Reveal.** When the condition is met, we publish the original and the verification data. Anyone can check that the revealed file produces the same commitment.

## 8 · ABOUT

### HERO

# Primitive started with a shortest-path problem.

It didn't begin as an AI company. The company came later.

---

I was working in mining when I got interested in a network problem. A physical mesh — components, possible routes, changing conditions — and a question that looked ordinary at first:

**What's the shortest path through this system?**

The longer I looked, the less ordinary it got. Shortest according to what — distance, time, cost, risk, energy, reliability? What happens when the state of one part changes the routes available elsewhere? What if the shortest path changes while you're travelling through it? What if the dimensions that matter aren't in the graph at all?

Eventually I started trying to imagine the geometry differently. Higher-dimensional coordinates. Neighbours that were adjacent in one state space and distant in another. Routes through state as well as space. Mostly I was finding out how badly I could hurt my own brain. But the question stayed.

---

### MEANWHILE, AT HOME

For about three years, Polina and I had an almost nightly habit of talking about whatever she was studying — psychology, philosophy, memory, learning, behaviour, identity, consciousness, emergence. Why people do what they do. Why behaviour changes with context. Why memory is reconstructive. How something as apparently unified as a person emerges from an enormous number of interacting processes.

Then Polina started working in behaviour support. And another problem showed up.

---

### COULD BEHAVIOUR BE REPRESENTED WELL ENOUGH TO PREDICT?

Years earlier I'd studied machine learning. I pulled the laptop back out and started trying to build a statistical prediction engine for behaviours of concern. It was wildly ambitious — which made it extremely useful, because human behaviour destroys naive models.

The same action can mean different things. Different internal states can produce the same visible behaviour. History matters. Environment matters. Relationships, sequence, timing — all of it matters. Observation changes interpretation. The boundary you draw around the system matters. And uncertainty is everywhere.

I kept hitting the same wall. Before I could ask *what will happen next?*, I had to answer something more fundamental: **what exactly am I representing?**

---

### THEN I RECOGNISED THE PATTERN

I'd seen it before. In the conveyor network. In shortest paths. In the behavioural model. In neural networks. Different problems, different domains, different terminology — and underneath all of them the same shape: things in states, capable of relationships, changing through interaction, forming recurring patterns, and sometimes producing patterns stable enough that we start treating the pattern itself as a thing.

That recognition was the beginning of Primitive. Not the company — the idea.

---

### WHY "PRIMITIVE"?

Because the work keeps returning to one question: *what's the smallest thing we need to assume?* Not the easiest thing to name. Not the first thing an observer sees. The primitive — the irreducible element you can build the useful description from.

Primitive doesn't mean unsophisticated. It means foundational.

---

### WHERE WE STAND

Primitive runs across three overlapping modes. **Research** — investigating representation, information, relationship and emergence. **Engineering** — turning those ideas into computational systems. **Applied work** — using the results on real operational problems. Each keeps the others honest: theory without implementation drifts, engineering without theory becomes accidental complexity, and commercial work without either becomes another wrapper. Primitive lives in the overlap.

---

### PRIMITIVE IS ONE OF THREE

The same systems thinking runs through everything we build, in three directions.

**Primitive** studies how complex systems work. **WHATBIT** fixes the part that matters. **Elsewhere** builds new systems in the physical world.

Down, in, out. One method, three moves.

> **Find the thing beneath the thing. Start there.**

## 9 · WHAT WE BELIEVE

### HERO

# What We Believe

Every technical system eventually rests on assumptions that aren't themselves technical. These are ours.

**01 · Information comes first.** In its most primitive sense: distinguishable state. Difference. Change. The possibility that one state could have been another.

**02 · A relationship can matter as much as the things it connects.** In many systems, relationships don't just describe objects — they constrain them, enable them, transform them, give them meaning.

**03 · Context changes state.** A description can be accurate in one frame and misleading in another. Time, observer, scale, boundary, purpose, environment. Context is not decoration.

**04 · Emergence isn't just a side effect.** If a recurring pattern gets stable enough for the rest of the system to treat it as a unit, that pattern has become operationally real. A representation should be able to account for it.

**05 · The observer is part of the description.** Boundaries are drawn from somewhere. Variables are chosen by someone. Measurements go through instruments. Every model contains perspective. Pretending otherwise doesn't remove it.

**06 · The map deserves scrutiny.** AI spends enormous effort evaluating models. The representations we hand those models deserve the same attention. A powerful reasoner over the wrong map still produces the wrong route.

**07 · Complexity must earn its place.** We like complicated ideas. We don't worship complicated implementations. If a linear model solves it, congratulations to the linear model.

**08 · Claims should carry labels.** Belief isn't evidence. A hypothesis isn't a result. A result isn't replication. Replication isn't universal truth. The distinctions matter.

**09 · Failure is information.** A failed experiment can improve the map. Hiding it can't.

**10 · Reality gets the final vote.** Always.

---

### THE MANIFESTO

We suspect reality is better understood as persistent patterns of changing state than as a collection of fundamentally isolated things. We suspect information, relationship and recurrence are deeper explanatory tools than our software architectures usually admit. We suspect these ideas can improve how intelligent systems represent and reason about complex environments.

Those are significant claims. Some are philosophical. Some may become scientific. Some may turn out to be wrong.

Our job isn't to protect them. Our job is to make them precise enough that reality can answer back.

## 17 · RESEARCH POLICY

# Research Policy

Primitive operates across commercial engineering, independent research and theoretical development. Those can conflict when the line between evidence and marketing goes blurry. This policy exists to keep that line visible.

**We label epistemic status.** Belief, hypothesis, experiment and replicated finding are not interchangeable.
**We preserve negative results.** A result shouldn't vanish because it became inconvenient.
**We version material changes.** Research evolves; the record should show how.
**We disclose methodological changes.** Changing an evaluation after seeing the result creates obvious problems. If a protocol changes, the change is visible.
**We publish enough to be criticised.** Where privacy, licensing, security and contract allow, research includes enough for an informed reader to inspect what was done.
**We distinguish internal from independent replication.** Running our own experiment again is useful. It is not independent validation.
**We don't use conviction as evidence.** Even when we really like the philosophy.
**We correct the record.** Visibly.
